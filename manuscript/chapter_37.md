# Chapter 37

## The Throne Room

---

The throne room was the silence made architectural.

Aelo entered through a corridor that had straightened as he walked, the Song's resonance peeling back decades of Varas's Molding, the stone remembering Fletcher's original design. The corridor opened onto the great hall, and the great hall was vast with a different kind of vastness: acoustic, oppressive. A room designed to hold the convergence of seven elemental memories, now holding nothing.

The walls were covered in Blood Vine, roots thicker here than anywhere else, the arterial network converging on a single point as rivers converge on a delta. The channels met and merged and descended into the floor, into the Chamber below, connecting the king on the throne to the prisoners in the dark.

Elder Stones everywhere. Dozens. Embedded in the walls, the floor, the throne itself, each one pulsing with the sickly amber of stolen magic. The Stones were _humming_, the low, operational frequency of a system in use.

Varas.

He was on the obsidian throne. Enormous. Not in height but in mass, in _density_, the body expanded by decades of consumption into something barely human. His skin had the texture of bark, the Blood Vine's growths having spread from his hands up his arms and across his torso and onto his face, the boundary between man and parasite dissolved. Extrusive. A man cooled too fast on the surface, the internal structure never given time to form. Obsidian made flesh. His hands were more Vine than flesh, the fingers thick, dark, joints swollen. His eyes were the color of Elder Stone — flat, lightless amber, so drained of warmth they read as stone.

He was connected to everything. The Vine ran from his body into the throne, from the throne into the floor, from the floor into the walls and the Stones and the network that extended through the castle and into the Murkr. He was the castle. The castle was him. The distinction had ceased to exist decades ago.

And he was smiling.

The smile was wrong. Because it was _genuine_. The smile of a man who has been waiting and the waiting is over.

"Finally," Varas said. His voice was soft, a voice that had no reason to be loud because the emptiness was the only audience it had ever had. "I've been waiting for someone who could make the stone sing."

Aelo stood in the doorway. The Song hummed in his blood, the full chord, all seven voices, involuntary, the blood responding to the proximity of the Elder Stones and the convergence point where the world's memory had been strongest.

Varas could feel it. Aelo could see it on the bark-textured face: the slight widening of the stone-colored eyes, the almost imperceptible tilt of the head.

"You're Fletcher's blood. I can feel it." The soft voice carried across the empty hall. The Blood Vine in the walls pulsed, the network responding to the king's attention, the organism orienting toward the stimulus. "You're humming — did you know that? Your blood is humming. It's the most beautiful sound I've ever almost heard."

"Almost?"

"I can't hear it. Not properly. I've never been able to hear it." Varas leaned forward on the throne. The movement was slow, the body's mass requiring effort, the bark-textured limbs heavy with decades of growth. "That was always the cruelty, wasn't it? Born into a world of music and deaf from the first breath."

---

Aelo walked into the hall. Toward the center, where the corridors converged, where the Blood Vine's channels met, where the floor vibrated with decades of extracted memory. He stood in the center and looked at the king and the king looked back.

"You killed people," Aelo said. "Thousands. You drained them. You put them in the Chamber and you connected them to your Stones and you consumed their memories until there was nothing left."

"Yes."

"You killed my mother."

"I ordered the nursery cleared. I did not know she would Sing. No one had Sung in generations. When the soldiers reported that a woman was holding a note that they could not pass — that their own blood was refusing to obey them — I was... interested." The stone eyes held a distance that was not emotional but structural. "I have spent my life being interested in the things I cannot feel. Your mother's Song was the most interesting thing that ever happened in my presence. I ordered them to wait. To let her Sing. I wanted to hear it."

"Did you?"

"Almost. The way I almost hear your blood. A vibration. A pressure against the barrier. The shape of a sound without the sound itself." He paused. The pause was not the Ming's deliberate interval or Jalo's defensive silence. It was the pause of a man who was describing the central condition of his existence and who was, for the first time, describing it to someone who might be capable of understanding. "She held the note for seven minutes. I listened for seven minutes. And for seven minutes, the barrier was thinner than it had ever been — thin enough that I could almost, _almost_ feel what was on the other side. And then the Molders broke through the wall and the note ended and the barrier returned and the almost was gone."

"And you've been trying to get it back ever since."

"I have been trying to get it back since I was born. The note was not the beginning. The note was the most recent failure in a lifetime of failures. Every person I drained, every Stone I fed from, every life I consumed — each one was an attempt. Each one gave me half a second. Half a second of almost-feeling, of the barrier thinning, of the ghost of an emotion pressing against the walls of a room that has no door. And then the half-second ended and I needed more."

He looked at Aelo. The stone eyes held the boy's face with the attention of a patient describing symptoms to the first doctor who has asked.

"I didn't want to be a monster," Varas said. Flat. Stated. As a man with a terminal diagnosis states the facts of his condition. "I wanted to hear the music. I took because I was never given. I drained because I could not fill. Every life I consumed, I got half a second of what you feel constantly — the world, speaking. And then it faded, and I needed more. And the more was never enough. And the never-enough was the hunger. And the hunger was the only thing I could feel."

"Then stop."

"I can't." Diagnosis, not defiance. "Stopping is silence. And silence is all I've ever known. You, with your gifts, your sensitivities, your tears over dead birds." The bark-textured face produced something between a smile and a grimace. "I know about the bird. The Guardian's memories — some reached me through the network. A boy, crying in a yard, holding a small dead thing. I consumed his memories for fifteen years and in every one of them, the love was the loudest thing."

The words landed with a weight Aelo had not expected.

"You felt it," Aelo said. "You felt his love."

"Almost. The way I almost feel everything. Enough to know it was there."

Aelo looked at the king. And for the first time, he did what his magic was born to do. He _felt_ Varas. The void. The hollow where a soul should be. The architectural absence that was not damage or choice but design — the building built without a door, the room without an exit, the life lived inside a silence so total that the silence had become the identity. Amorphous glass, vibrating against crystal after crystal, unable to resonate with any of them.

Varas was in pain. A pain so absolute, the pain of being excluded from existence itself, that it had warped him into the thing he was. The Chamber. The draining. The Dog Scene. The purge. All of it was _reaching_. The four-year-old boy in Aldric's memory, hands up, wanting to be held. Still reaching. Still unable to close the gap.

"I'm sorry," Aelo said.

Varas blinked. The stone eyes, which had not changed expression since Aelo entered the room, changed. Not dramatically. A fractional adjustment. The muscles around the eyes contracting by a millimeter. Involuntary. Uncontrollable. A person spoken to with a word they have never heard directed at them before.

In forty years of rule, through executions and drainings and the feast and the purge and the Chamber, no one had ever said that to him.

"Don't," Varas said. His voice cracked. Barely. A hairline fracture. "Don't you dare."

"I'm going to Sing."

"I know."

"It will undo everything you've built."

"I know."

"The draining will reverse. The stolen memories will return. The Murkr will heal. And you —" Aelo looked at the man on the throne. "You will lose everything."

"Everything I have is stolen. Losing it is not a loss. It's an accounting."

"You could stop me."

"I could." Varas was quiet. The Blood Vine pulsed, the network awaiting instruction, the castle's immune system poised to attack. The king could give the order. The Song in the boy's blood would be drained and the draining would give Varas perhaps a full second of almost-hearing.

"But then I'd never hear it," Varas said.

The silence held. He was not redeemed. The sorry had not reached him. The boy's compassion had not pierced the void. The void was so vast that even the man who had caused all of this would rather hear one note of the Song and die than live another day in silence.

"Sing," Varas said. "Let me hear it. One note. One real note. Not the ghost. Not the almost. The thing itself. Let me hear what the world sounds like to everyone except me."

He closed his stone eyes. His hands relaxed on the armrests. The Blood Vine stilled, the network standing down, the castle ceasing its defense. The king surrendered to the need. The first need. The only need. The need to hear.

Aelo looked at the king who had destroyed the world to fill a silence that could not be filled. The stone eyes closed, the bark-textured face tilted upward, waiting for a sound he had waited his entire life to hear.

"You'll hear it," Aelo said. "I promise."

He turned toward the corridor that led down, to the Chamber, to the prisoners, to where Sereth was waiting and the Song was about to be Sung.

Behind him, Varas waited. The first patient thing the man had done in seventy years.
