# Chapter 39

## The Conduit

---

Sereth reached the Chamber on Laine's back, and the Chamber remembered her.

The Blood Vine recoiled. The Elder Stones hummed with recognition, amber light brightening for a moment. The walls, the floor — the original Fletcher-era masonry, responded with the deep, geological recognition of stone encountering the frequency it had been designed to resonate with.

Laine set Sereth down. The old body was fragile, the golden eye dimming toward brown. She stood. The standing cost her.

The prisoners. The rows of platforms. The open eyes.

Sereth's true face produced an expression that came from a time before emotions had names. The expression of a being seeing what humanity had done with her gift.

"I did this," Sereth said. "Every Stone in this room, every Vine, every draining mechanism — it's all based on what I gave to a man who wanted to help the world remember."

"You couldn't have known."

"I knew. I gave it anyway because the alternative was silence, and silence was worse." She looked at the prisoners. "This can be undone."

She began unwinding the Blood Vine from the prisoners.

With her hands. The nearly-mortal hands, the grip measured in human force. She took the first tendril and pulled — gently, as you remove a thorn from a child's palm. The Vine resisted. It had grown into the skin, threaded through nerve pathways. Pulling it free was not cutting — it was _unweaving_.

Laine joined. Together, they worked through the row — prisoner by prisoner, tendril by tendril.

Each freed prisoner gasped. The single memory flared — the kiss, the laugh, the rain, each one brightening, guttering, then steadying. Small but stable. The fuel no longer being consumed.

The prisoners were not restored. The draining had taken too much. But they were disconnected. Free.

---

Sereth connected the Blood Vine to herself.

She took the severed tendrils and gathered them. One by one. Each tendril placed against her own skin — her chest, her arms. The Vine attached. The tendrils penetrated. The connection established.

Sereth breathed through it. The Vine's draw was immediate, automatic. But the divine memory it found was not personal — it was foundational. The memory of the world's beginning. The Vine drew. Sereth held. The two forces balanced in sustained, agonizing equilibrium.

"What will it cost you?" Laine's voice was tight.

"Everything I was." The golden eye found Laine's face. "But I haven't been a god in a very long time. Now I'll just be a person."

She turned to the locked door. Opened it with her own hand — the stone remembered _her_.

She touched Aldric's face. The king's eyes focused for the first time in three decades on a face that was not his captor's.

"I'm sorry, old friend," Sereth said. "I should have come sooner."

Aldric's lips moved. One word, produced by a throat that had to remember, muscle by muscle, how to shape air into sound: "Finally."

Sereth connected Aldric and Vero to the network. When the Song came, it would reach them too.

---

Aelo entered the Chamber.

He stood in the doorway and saw: Sereth at the center, wrapped in Blood Vine, the golden eye nearly brown. The prisoners disconnected, their single memories flickering. Aldric and Vero connected through the conduit. Laine at the door, tears on her face.

He walked to the center. He stood beside Sereth.

"I don't know how to Sing," Aelo said.

Sereth looked at him. The golden-brown eye held the boy's face with three centuries of watching Fletcher's descendants fail and die and try again.

"Yes you do," Sereth said. "You've been Singing your whole life. Every time you felt someone's pain. Every time you heard Jalo's nightmares through the wall. Every time you cried over a dead bird in the yard. The Song isn't a technique. It's love made audible."

The Blood Vine pulsed. The prisoners' single memories flickered, each one a candle waiting for the wind that would fan it into something brighter.

Laine stood at the door. She closed her eyes. She did not pray. She did something harder. She believed.

The Chamber waited. The conduit hummed. The boy stood at the center.

The Song was about to begin.
