# Chapter 36

## The Arrival

---

Sereth entered the Murkr in her true form, and the Murkr tried to erase her.

The fisherman was gone. The old woman was gone. Every borrowed face — gone. What remained was the original shape, and the original shape was less than the stories described. Gods looked like their choices, and Sereth's choices had made her look like a person who was very tired and very old and very nearly finished.

She was small. Compact, worn, the surface weathered by divinity leaving flesh. One eye. The left was gone, the price of giving humanity the Song. The remaining eye was the color of the world's first light, a gold so deep it was almost amber.

The Murkr hit her like a wall.

Each step cost her something. The color of the sky the day she gave Fletcher the gift, gone. The sound of the other gods' voices, gone. The feeling of being infinite, gone, and what remained was the mortal's perspective: linear, limited, moving through time rather than containing it.

She did not care. The caring had been the first thing mortality gave her and it was worth more than the infinity had ever been.

She walked. The grey pressed. The divine memory fell away.

---

She found Laine at the border.

Unconscious. The Vine-burns on her legs dark, memory injuries, the flesh damaged by extraction rather than heat.

Sereth knelt beside her. She looked at Laine's hands. Small, angular, callused from years of coastal work.

Essa's hands. The same shape. Three hundred years and the hands had survived, the genetic memory carried through generations of daughters, arriving here, in this woman, at this moment.

Sereth allowed herself one moment of grief. She looked at Essa's hands on Laine's wrists and remembered: braiding rope on a dock in Bahar. Cupping water from a stream. On Sereth's face, the night Essa understood what she was and chose to love her anyway. Old and spotted, the last time Sereth held them.

One moment. Then the work began.

She Healed her. The last significant act of magic she might ever perform. She placed her hands on Laine's legs and _remembered_ — the divine version. The original act of mending that had existed before the disciplines were divided. The direct, god-to-flesh restoration of a body's memory of wholeness, performed through the fading authority of a being that had been present when the body was first designed.

The burns healed. The dark marks faded. The memory-damage repaired. Enough to restore consciousness. Enough to give the Guide-sight a chance.

The healing cost her. She felt the last measurable reserve of divinity depart through her hands and disappear. What remained was a person. Very old, very tired, with one eye and a purpose clearer than it had been in three centuries.

Laine's eyes opened. The Guide eyes were different. Still, for the first time. The stillness of eyes that had been shattered and were reassembling with a clarity the constant searching had never permitted.

She saw Sereth. The true face, tipping toward human, the gold eye dimming toward amber.

"Who are you?"

"Someone who has been watching over your family for three hundred years." Sereth's voice was thinner in the true form. Quieter. "And I need your help."

Laine's Guide-sight — damaged, reassembling, operating at a fraction of its former capacity, reached outward. A few hundred yards, no more. The coastal clarity that had read trajectories from miles away was gone. Enough to feel the castle. Enough to feel the boy inside it.

"He's going to Sing," she said.

"Yes."

"He's going to die."

"He's going to become something else. Whether that's death depends on how much help he has."

---

Sereth told her the truth. Quickly. The raw facts.

Who she was: one of the seven gods. The one who had loved humanity enough to give them the Song and had been punished for it.

What the Song was: love made audible. The harmonization of all seven disciplines. Fletcher had Sung and ceased. Maera had held five notes and died.

What she needed: Laine to carry her into the Chamber. Sereth's body was failing in the Murkr. But once inside, she could serve as a conduit. A bridge between Aelo and the prisoners.

"The Blood Vine feeds on infrastructure I built," Sereth said. "The channels through which magic flows, the pathways connecting the Elder Stones — I designed them. The Vine burrowed into my roads. It feeds on a system it did not create and does not understand."

She paused. The effort of speaking was visible — the true body diminishing with each sentence.

"A builder can change the direction of flow in her own architecture. The Vine drains because it follows the current. I'm going to remember the current flowing the other way — outward instead of inward, giving instead of taking. The Vine has no will. It's a mouth. I'm going to point it the other direction."

"Fletcher Sang alone and it cost him everything. I can change that. I connect myself to the Vine network. Aelo Sings. The Song flows into me. I channel it through the reversed Vine to every prisoner, every Stone, every node. The cost gets distributed across a thousand vessels instead of landing on one boy."

"Will he survive?"

"He'll survive. But he'll be diminished. The Song will take memories. Identity. He'll live in a world that's slightly less vivid than the one he remembers."

"And you?"

Sereth smiled, something older and simpler than any borrowed expression, carrying the warmth of a person who has made peace with what's coming.

"When the Song passes through me, whatever divinity remains will burn away. I'll become mortal. I'll age. I'll die. Like Essa."

"That doesn't sound like a cost."

The golden eye held Laine's gaze. The light in it was dimming, and the dimming was not loss but completion.

"It is what I wanted all along," Sereth said. "I just needed a reason to let it happen."

They entered the Murkr together. Laine carrying Sereth, the old body lighter than it should have been, more memory than mass. A human carrying the last god toward the last Song. The grey pressed. The divine memory fell.
